# Interview questions

This page lists common interview questions for technical writers. It describes what interviewers usually look for, so that you can prepare your own examples. Do not memorize answers. Use your own projects, and describe what you did and why.

**Last checked:** November 2026. Interview formats differ between companies.

---

## Process and craft

| Question | What interviewers look for |
|---|---|
| Walk me through how you write a complex technical document. | A clear process, from research and outlining to editing and review |
| How do you keep technical details accurate? | Verification against sources, testing of steps, and review by subject matter experts |
| How do you decide how much detail to include? | Thinking about the reader's task and what they already know |

---

## Audience and structure

| Question | What interviewers look for |
|---|---|
| How do you present information to different audiences? | Separate paths for beginners and experts, such as a guide and a reference |
| How do you organize a large documentation set? | Clear navigation, consistent structure, and findable content |
| How do you write for a reader who is new to the product? | Plain language, defined terms, and examples |

---

## Tools and standards

| Question | What interviewers look for |
|---|---|
| Which documentation tools have you used? | Honest experience, and an understanding of trade-offs |
| What do you know about structured authoring, such as XML or DITA? | Understanding of when structure helps, and its cost |
| Which style guide do you follow? | Familiarity with a standard, and how you apply it consistently |
| How do you use Git and pull requests for documentation? | Familiarity with a review workflow, and version history |

---

## Collaboration and pressure

| Question | What interviewers look for |
|---|---|
| How do you work with busy subject matter experts? | Clear questions, short sessions, and written follow-up |
| How do you work within an Agile process? | Planning docs with the sprint, not after it |
| How do you protect quality when a deadline is tight? | Priorities, a minimum standard, and a clear trade-off |

---

## AI and changing tools

| Question | What interviewers look for |
|---|---|
| How do you use AI in your documentation work? | Clear tasks where AI helps, and the checks you apply |
| How do you check AI-generated content? | Verification against sources, and a human review step |
| How do you learn a new tool? | A method, such as a small project or a trial page |

---

## Behavioral questions

Use the STAR method for behavioral questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

| Question | Structure your answer |
|---|---|
| Tell me about a time you disagreed with a reviewer. | Situation: the disagreement. Task: what you needed. Action: how you resolved it. Result: the outcome. |
| Describe a documentation error that reached readers. | Situation: the error. Action: how you found and fixed it. Result: what you changed to prevent it. |

---

## Preparing your answers

- Choose two or three projects, and prepare each one in STAR format.
- Bring a writing sample, and be ready to explain your choices.
- Practice a short rewrite of a technical paragraph for a beginner audience.
- Prepare two questions for the interviewer about their documentation process.

:::note

Some interview guides online use sample answers written by AI or by coaches. Use them to understand the question, and write your own answer from your own experience.

:::
